# Check history in three tiers, sealed days as packed rows, uptime from outages, and a size budget

Check history lives in three tiers bounded by one retention setting: raw checks for the smaller of 30 days and the retention, hourly rollups for the smaller of 90 days and the retention, daily rollups to the retention limit. The retention setting has an install-wide default of 90 days and a per-monitor override. Raw checks are written as plain rows into a head table keyed by monitor and time; a nightly job seals each closed day into one packed row per monitor, compressed with Bun's zstd at level 3, writes that day's hourly and daily rollup rows, and deletes the head rows. Uptime is duration-based from the outage records, never from check counts. A maximum database size setting, default unlimited, bounds Raaqeb's own growth by deleting the oldest history before a write.

Measured under Bun 1.4.2 with 50 monitors for 30 days at a 60 second interval (decisive numbers re-run on a one-monitor probe), with an integer monitor id: plain rows with an index cost 46 bytes per check, a WITHOUT ROWID table 28, a sealed day 7; the 30-day chart read 2 times faster from sealed days, the hourly rollup 22 times faster, and the purge of a day 40 times faster. zstd levels 1 to 19 and gzip landed within 0.4 percent of each other, so the packing does the work and the level is fixed. Disk-full behaviour was run on both backends.

## Decisions bundled here

- **Tiers and windows.** Raw for the smaller of 30 days and the monitor's retention, hourly for the smaller of 90 days and that retention, daily to the retention limit. Lowering a setting takes effect at the next nightly job, which honours per-monitor overrides. Daily rows cost about 36 KB per monitor per year, so a retention of years is cheap.
- **Head table key.** The head table's primary key is the pair (monitor, started at), with no autonumber column. On SQLite the migration SQL writes `WITHOUT ROWID` by hand, because Drizzle's table builder has no option for it; on Postgres the same composite key sits over a plain heap. The difference is physical and invisible to the operator. The monitor id in the key is the text UUIDv7, so a head row measures about 68 bytes rather than the 28 to 46 above, which assume an integer id; the head table holds one day, so the text key was kept over an integer alias column.
- **Day boundaries are UTC** everywhere: sealing, rollup rows, the cells of the day bar and the memo's date. The install time zone only renders timestamps.
- **Sealed days.** A closed UTC day becomes one row per monitor holding every check with every field, packed column by column (delta-of-delta timestamps, delta response times and byte counts, raw bytes for result, reason, attempts and status) and compressed with zstd level 3. No setting for the level. The blob carries a format version byte. Reading a day decodes it in under a millisecond; fetching one old check decodes that monitor's day.
- **Sealing job.** Idempotent, runs after a day is safely closed (a check timestamped 23:59:58 can be written at 00:00:03), commits the chunk before deleting the head rows, and writes the day's hourly and daily rollup rows from the same data. It runs on the main event loop in per-monitor batches that yield between monitors, so a pass never holds the loop against a due check. The accelerated day in the floor benchmark includes a sealing pass. Today is read live from the head rows; no rollup row exists for the open day.
- **Rollup contents.** Check count, observed time, average, minimum and maximum response time (of the up and degraded checks), failed and degraded counts. Observed time is stored in milliseconds, like every other duration. The sealed-day header carries observed time too. Both are computed from that day's raw checks at sealing. No percentiles in v1.
- **Uptime.** One minus outage seconds inside the window over observed seconds inside it. Maintenance windows, paused time and any gap with no check for longer than twice the monitor's interval are no data and leave the denominator. An outage starts at the first failed check and ends at the first good one; the interface says "detected at" and shows the interval as the resolution. Inside the raw window the denominator is computed from the checks; beyond it, uptime reads the hourly rollups' observed time, and an hour's downtime is its outage time capped at that observed time. The sealing job leaves the time maintenance windows covered a monitor out of its sealed day's and rollups' observed time, through the component links as they are when it seals; the checks keep their true results.
- **Two uptime numbers.** A monitor's uptime is the rule above. A component's uptime, and each cell of its day bar, is the union of the outage time of its linked monitors and the time incidents held it at partial or major outage, read from the outage records and the incident update history. Degraded performance set by an incident is not downtime, as a degraded monitor is not. The per-page "include maintenance" toggle is on the deferred list.
- **Chart tiers.** The response-time chart reads raw checks, head rows plus sealed days, for the 24 hour and 7 day ranges, hourly rollups for 30 and 90 days, and daily rollups beyond.
- **Displayed numbers never flatter.** Uptime is rounded down, with adaptive decimals from 2 up to 5 so a window with any outage never reads as 100%. The hover shows exact downtime in seconds and the observed time. 100% appears only when no outage was recorded.
- **Day bar.** Each day coloured in steps by its uptime from outage records, hover shows the percentage and that day's outages, today's cell live.
- **Failure details and captures** keep the raw window and are deleted by the same nightly job as their day's chunk.
- **Size budget.** A maximum database size setting, default unlimited. With a number set, Raaqeb checks used size before writes and deletes the oldest history first: raw days, then hourly rows, then daily rows; never outages, incidents, certificate records or settings. Used size is SQLite's pages in use and Postgres's own tables. The nightly purge and every size-budget delete bump the change counter, so no memoised public view is built on history that is already gone. A local SQLite file keeps a 64 MB reserve of free disk, with or without a number set, and a check the reserve cannot make room for is not written. Postgres exposes no free-space or quota signal, so with no number set it runs unlimited and the settings page says so. Reacting to the full-disk error was measured and does nothing on either backend.
- **Space back to the disk.** SQLite runs with incremental vacuum after the nightly delete, followed by a checkpoint; Raaqeb caps its own write-ahead log at 4 MB by checkpoint setting. Postgres's plain vacuum reuses space inside the file only; logged, not worked around.
- **Interval changes** need nothing special. The interval is shown on the monitor page.
- **Export.** One file, `raaqeb-export.jsonl.zst`: JSON Lines under zstd from Bun's own API. The first line is the configuration object, each following line one record tagged with its kind and monitor (check, hourly, daily, outage), streamed both ways so a large history never sits in memory. Configuration-only export is the first line alone. Full export carries the finest kind available per range and outages in full; sealed days are decoded to plain check records. A per-monitor download for one range is the same JSON Lines, a convenience and not the import format. The configuration object carries channel credentials decrypted, and import re-encrypts them with the target install's key, so install keys never travel and the download page says the file contains secrets. The database URL does not travel. Subscriptions and the settings that travel are in the configuration. Import refuses an install holding monitors, status pages, components, incidents or maintenance windows.

## Considered options

- **Plain rows only, WITHOUT ROWID.** 28 bytes per check and no job. Rejected for 4 times the disk of sealed days and slower charts, rollups and purges; kept as the shape of the head table.
- **sqlite-zstd extension.** Transparent row compression with trained dictionaries. Loaded under bun:sqlite, then grew the database from 105 MB to 136 MB, made the 30-day query 15 times slower, is LGPLv3, ships no arm64 Linux build, and is SQLite-only, which breaks parity. Rejected.
- **Postgres TOAST or columnar extensions.** TOAST never touches a row under 2 KB, measured byte-identical with and without lz4. TimescaleDB, Citus columnar and Hydra are self-managed or vendor-cloud add-ons. Rejected.
- **Hourly chunks.** 17 percent more disk and 41 percent slower on the 30-day chart than daily. Rejected.
- **Count-based uptime.** Distorts when the interval changes and needs up and down fields in every rollup. Rejected for duration from outages.
- **React to the full-disk error.** On SQLite every delete, checkpoint and journal-mode switch failed with the same error at zero bytes free; on Postgres the server PANICked and could not finish crash recovery. Rejected for checking before writes.
- **Export as one JSON document.** A 50-monitor, 30-day history is about 220 MB of JSON and over 1 GB to parse in Bun. Rejected for JSON Lines. An archive with per-monitor files was considered and dropped as an unneeded second layout.

## Consequences

- Every read of check history has two sources, the head table and the sealed days, and merges at the day boundary. The most frequent query, latest check per monitor, reads only the head; for a monitor with no head row, paused or not yet checked today, it reads the last check of the newest sealed day.
- The packed blob is an on-disk format Raaqeb owns and must version; a decoder for every past version ships forever.
- Rollup rows and sealed-day headers carry observed time and no uptime field; uptime code divides the outage seconds from the outage records by that observed time, and reads maintenance windows for what to leave out.
- The size budget is a self-limit. Neither backend survives another process filling the disk; the documentation says so.
- Differences between the backends found here: two-argument min and least, no small-row compression, `max_page_count`, incremental vacuum versus VACUUM FULL, and `pg_database_size` readable remotely.
